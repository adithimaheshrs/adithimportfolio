Chapter 01

Context — the
quiet problem.

— Why the loudest moment of the day was hiding inside a meditation app.

— brief
A wind-down app that wouldn't wind down.

The brief was small on paper: redesign the evening flow of a meditation app whose 8 PM retention had dropped 34% in six months. Users were opening the app, then closing it within ninety seconds.

What I found instead was a familiar product pattern dressed in soft colors — streaks, notification badges, a home tab competing for attention. The app was structurally a feed. The feature it was meant to support — a slow ritual at the end of the day — had been buried under three taps and a recommendation engine.

So the work became less about adding screens and more about removing the parts that made the app feel like work. A redesign aimed at one question: what does software look like when it isn't trying to keep you?

“The first screen of a wind-down app should not feel like a homepage. It should feel like turning down a lamp.”
— Project notes, week 02
Chapter 02

Discovery — twenty
evenings, in.

— Diaries, drop-off tracking, and a tea kettle that ran the show.

— research
Twenty diaries. Five interviews. One pattern.

I recruited twelve participants for a two-week evening diary study and ran five one-hour interviews. Each evening, participants logged what they reached for, what they avoided, and the moment they felt the day had ended.

The pattern was loud: the ritual people described — tea, a book, dim lights — had nothing to do with the app's structure. The app was a stop between Netflix and bed. It needed to be a stop worth taking.

The most useful finding was unsentimental: people don't open a wind-down app to find something. They open it because they've already chosen, and they want the chosen thing to begin.

Chapter 03

Synthesis —
three principles.

— What the redesign would have to be true to.

— principles
Three rules I held the work to.

Synthesis is mostly editing. I wrote forty insight cards down to three short principles — the kind of sentences that survive a design review at 5 PM.

PRINCIPLE 01

Begin where
the user begins.

Open the app on the chosen ritual, not the choosing surface. Save the library for a deliberate detour, not the default.

PRINCIPLE 02

Subtract
before adding.

For every screen, remove one element. No streaks, no badges, no progress bars — nothing that turns a ritual into a metric.

PRINCIPLE 03

Hold the room
quiet.

Use type, timing, and color the way a host uses light — to set the temperature, not to direct traffic.

Chapter 04

The design
— small & slow.

— Two screens, a colour shift after sunset, and exactly zero badges.

— design
Two screens did most of the work.

The redesign is small. A first screen that names the time of day, a single rounded card with tonight's ritual, and a quiet shelf of three alternates a scroll away. The home tab is the ritual. The library is the detour.

Chapter 05

Outcome — &
what I'd change.

— Small numbers, useful ones, and the part I'd revisit.

— reflection
A pseudo project, real lessons.

A four-week usability round with seven participants tested the new flow against the original. Numbers below are small-sample and held loosely — but the direction was clear, and the qualitative feedback was the kind I actually wanted.

The thing I'd change: I treated the library as a detour, but two participants used it as their primary entrance. A next iteration would let the home screen remember that — opening on a chosen surface, not a default one.
