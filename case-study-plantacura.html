Chapter 01

The problem.

— Care information exists. It just doesn't fit real life.

— the problem
Easy to love, hard to keep alive.

Plants are easy to love and surprisingly hard to keep alive. Life gets busy, watering schedules slip, and most people genuinely don't know that different plants have completely different needs. You can't water a succulent like a fern. You can't leave a monstera in a dark corner and expect it to thrive. And when you go on a two-week holiday, your entire balcony can wilt before you're back.

PlantaCura started from a simple observation: plant care information exists everywhere, but it's scattered, generic, and doesn't fit into the way people actually live.

This project was built as part of the Google UX Design Certificate, my first structured end-to-end design process, and the one that cemented how I think about user research.

Chapter 02

Understanding
the problem.

— Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test.

— research
Overwhelmed, not careless.

I followed the full design thinking process, Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test, and started by actually talking to and surveying real plant owners.

Quantitative research via a Google Form with around 30 responses gave me a clear picture fast. More than half of respondents had no proper watering routine, the single most essential thing for keeping a plant alive.

50%+
had no proper watering routine.
70%
had plants in pots, which need more attention than ground-planted ones.

Most were clueless when choosing a plant for a specific room. More than two thirds didn't know how to care for specific plant types, and either treated everything the same or only paid attention when something was visibly wrong.

Four pain points crystallised from the research: people couldn't maintain a consistent care routine, couldn't match plants to their room conditions, had no support for plant care while travelling, and couldn't identify diseases or pests attacking their plants.

Chapter 03

Who I designed
for.

— Two people, one shared overwhelm.

— personas
They want the routine.
PERSONA 01

Rita, 28, strategist

Based in Mumbai, travels frequently for work. Loves her balcony plants and finds their care calming, but comes home from trips to find wilted or dead plants. Has multiple small plants with different watering needs and no way to track them, and is frustrated by contradictory advice online.

PERSONA 02

Kaira & Sahil, game developers

A couple in Delhi with cats and a genuine concern about plant toxicity. Want air-purifying plants to combat Delhi's pollution, but worry about their pets getting poisoned. Demanding schedules mean they keep accidentally over or under-watering.

The empathy map built from these users revealed something important: plant owners aren't careless, they're overwhelmed. They want to take care of their plants. They maintain journals, visit nurseries, research care instructions. The joy of having plants is real. The system around supporting that joy simply doesn't exist.

The user journey

Mapping the user journey made the design opportunities concrete. A user trying to remember when to water their plant makes a mental note, waits for the soil to look right, then either gets it right or watches the plant wilt. When something goes wrong, a bad fertiliser choice, a pest attack, they're googling frantically or calling a nursery, often too late.

The opportunities that emerged were clear: a plant journal to track care and growth, precise watering reminders based on plant type, a way to arrange care for plants during travel, and a way to identify and address plant disease.

Chapter 04

What I designed.

— A plant journal and care companion.

— design
Care that fits real life.

PlantaCura is a plant journal and care companion app built around four core features.

  • My Plants
    A personal plant library where you add each plant, select its species, and choose a care routine. The app knows that your monstera needs water every 7 days and your succulent every 14. You don't have to remember.
  • Personalised reminders
    Timely notifications for watering, nutrient spraying, and repotting, tailored to each plant's actual needs rather than a generic schedule.
  • Plant scanner
    Point your camera at a plant to identify it, diagnose potential diseases or pest problems, and get specific treatment guidance. No more guessing.
  • Community care
    Find and connect with nearby plant lovers who can look after your plants while you're travelling, based on location, with messaging built in.
  • Blog and search
    Plant care articles, tips, and a search function so knowledge is always a tap away rather than a Google rabbit hole.

The user flow covered the full journey from onboarding through to every key feature: login, register, home, scan, add a plant, select a care routine, add a blog, search, and message.

— reflection
Fit the behaviour, don't fight it.

This was one of my earliest formal design projects, completed as part of the Google UX Design Certificate. It taught me the value of quantitative research done early: the survey data I collected in the first week shaped every design decision that followed. Without it, I would have built around assumptions.

It also taught me that the most useful apps don't try to change user behaviour. PlantaCura works because it fits around how plant owners already think and act: they already want routines, they already visit nurseries, they already love their plants. The app just makes all of that easier.
